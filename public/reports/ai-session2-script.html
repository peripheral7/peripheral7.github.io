PART 3. 프랑스 사례: 국제학은 어떻게 쓰이는가 약 4.8분

[Slide 4 — Student protests]

Let's say I want to write about France. This is just an example to show how international studies can be applied to things happening in the world.

[Slide 5 — France: three areas (cards)]

This situation is related to three areas we learn in our department: economics, political theories, and business.

[Slide 6 — Economics ①: government balance, social protection, pensions]

First, economics. The French government has run a deficit every year since 2000. In 2024 it was 5.8% of GDP, far below the EU limit of minus 3%. A big reason is social spending. France spends 23% of GDP on social protection, and pensions alone cost 13.6% of GDP, the highest of these four countries.

[Slide 7 — Economics ②: real GDP per capita, productivity]

At the same time, growth is weak. Since 2000, real GDP per capita has grown 20% in France, compared with 25% in Germany and 40% in the US. Behind this is slower productivity growth, which makes it harder to pay for a large welfare state.

[Slide 8 — International Relations]

From the welfare state model in Contemporary Theories of IR, France is spending too much on social protection and pensions. Fiscal stress and political deadlock can increase support for illiberal or populist parties. That is a hypothesis we can test with IR theories.

[Slide 9 — Business Finance: France–Germany bond yield spread]

Last but not least is business finance. A bond rate consists of common interest rate factors such as maturity, expected inflation, and real rate. On top of that is the spread. Each country has different risk and different credibility, and it makes the country-specific premium. This premium also includes political risk, so the political instability we discuss in IR shows up directly in the interest rate. We compare France with Germany because German government bonds are the euro area's benchmark: Germany has a AAA credit rating and one of the lowest default risks, and its bonds trade in a deep, liquid market, so the gap shows France-specific risk. Over the past year, the France–Germany 10-year spread averaged 0.77 percentage points, and on 20 October it reached 1.52, its highest in the past year.

[Slide 10 — Through which framework are we looking?]

So if you want to write well, you have to have a framework you are looking through.

PART 4. AI를 글쓰기에 쓰는 세 단계 약 1.7분

Now I'll show you three steps of using AI when writing an essay.

[Slides 12–13 — Step 1: Research (News, Literature)]

Step 1: Research. You can connect the current situation with other areas, and check whether they are directly related. It is also a good idea to look for recent literature.

[Slide 14 — Step 2: Data Processing & Visualization]

Step 2: Data processing and visualization. In 2022, I had to check the Excel file and write the Python code by myself. Nowadays, it takes just one minute to make a graph.

[Slide 15 — Step 3: Fact-Check & Articulation]

Step 3: Fact-check and articulation. You can use AI to fact-check or restructure your writing.

[Slide 15 (계속) — You verify and decide (전용 슬라이드 없음)]

Through all three steps, you are the one who verifies and decides. AI helps with finding, processing, and checking, but we are the ones who should be thinking.
