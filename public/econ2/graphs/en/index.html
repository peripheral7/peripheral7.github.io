    Ch30 Money Growth and InflationMoney market: value of money 1/P

    M × V = P × Y Value of money = 1 / P MS is set by the Fed, so it is vertical. MD slopes down: the lower the value of money (the higher P), the more money people need. With V · Y fixed, P rises in proportion to M (quantity theory, monetary neutrality). Fisher effect: i = r + π Nominal interest rate i = real interest rate r + inflation rate π. In the long run, faster money growth raises π, and i rises one for one; r is unchanged.

              Ch33 Aggregate SupplyWhat shifts AS

              Short run: Y = Y* + a(P − Pe) Long run: Y* = A · F(L, K, H, N) Y* = natural level of output, Pe = expected price level, A = technology. Pe, nominal wages and input costs shift only SRAS; labor, capital, resources and technology shift LRAS (and SRAS). The money supply cannot move LRAS.
              Why SRAS slopes upward

              All three theories say output departs from Y* when P differs from Pe. If P falls below Pe:

              1. Sticky-wage theory — nominal wages were set on Pe, so real wages are too high → firms hire less → Y ↓.
              2. Sticky-price theory — menu costs keep some prices from falling → those firms' prices are too high → sales ↓ → Y ↓.
              3. Misperceptions theory — suppliers mistake the fall in P for a fall in their relative price → they produce less → Y ↓.

              In the long run wages, prices and perceptions adjust (Pe catches up with P), so Y returns to Y* and LRAS is vertical.

                  Ch28 Unemployment · Labor marketWage above equilibrium → unemployment

                  Unemployment = labor supplied LS − labor demanded LD At the equilibrium wage WE labor supplied equals labor demanded, so this graph shows no unemployment. Minimum wages, unions and efficiency wages hold the wage above WE, leaving the gap as unemployment (structural unemployment).
                  Unemployment even at WE: frictional

                  Frictional unemployment — workers take time to search for jobs that fit them, even when the wage is at WE. Sectoral shifts (demand moving between industries) and unemployment insurance lengthen the search.

                  Natural rate = frictional + structural unemployment, the level the economy normally has. Cyclical unemployment = the deviation from the natural rate over the business cycle.

                    Ch28 Unemployment · Labor statisticsUnemployment rate · Participation rate

                    Unemployment rate = Unemployed ÷ Labor force Participation rate = Labor force ÷ Adult population Labor force = Employed + Unemployed. Adult population = Employed + Unemployed + Not in labor force. People not looking for work (including discouraged workers) count as not in the labor force, not as unemployed.
